I am a product-minded engineer and technical product leader based in Lausanne. I have been shipping software since 2008, when the first version of studenti.rs went live: agency and client work after that, then IoT platforms, then AR/VR, then consumer scale at Shopify, and now product ownership at ZF SCALAR alongside the independent products I build and run.

The through-line is not a technology. It is that I keep moving toward the part of the problem nobody owns — the tooling everyone works around, the duplication nobody is paid to remove, the market with no usable product in it — and then owning it.

Company work shaped the systems. Independent work sharpened the judgment. Neither would be enough on its own: employment taught me what quality means at scale and how organisations actually adopt things, and my own products taught me what happens when there is nobody else to hand the hard half to.

## How I work

I start from the user and the constraint, not from the architecture. Most technical disagreements dissolve once everyone agrees on what has to be true for the person using the thing.

I stay hands-on. I have led teams and owned roadmaps, and in both cases the decisions got worse when I lost contact with the code. I do not need to write all of it, but I need to be able to judge the cost of a trade-off without asking.

I use AI as a parallel execution system: research, planning, implementation, review, operations and support. Agents run ingestion health checks, moderation queues, dependency updates and reporting on my own products. The skill is not prompting. It is structuring context, designing verification and keeping ownership of the decision. AI changed the economics of building; it did not remove the need for judgment.

I write things down. Decisions with reasons attached survive contact with new people; decisions in someone's head do not.

## Principles

- Adoption is the deliverable. A platform nobody uses is a liability with good test coverage.
- Developer experience is product work. It has users, a roadmap and a retention problem.
- Ship the smallest thing that tells you something true, then keep the feedback loop short.
- Be specific in public. Vague claims are unfalsifiable, and unfalsifiable claims are worthless to the person reading them.
- Automate operations, not judgment. Write the policy first; then let a machine apply it.
- Say no with the reason attached. A no without one is just friction.
- Prefer boring infrastructure. Interesting infrastructure is a second product you did not plan to maintain.

## Tools

TypeScript everywhere. React and React Native for products, Next.js and Astro for the web, Node.js on the server, Postgres for anything that matters. Cloudflare at the edge, Hetzner for the machines I own, Vercel where it fits.

Claude Code and Cursor for day-to-day building, with local models for data processing that should not leave the machine. PostHog for product analytics, GitHub Actions for anything that should happen without me. Long-running agents for operations across studenti.rs and TenderLift.

Design systems, Storybook, accessibility work and the unglamorous tooling around all of it.
