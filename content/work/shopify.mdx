## Summary

I spent a little over two years at Shopify, most of it on Shop Minis: an exploration that became a mini-app ecosystem embedded inside Shop.app. It started as a prototype with a small team and ended as a platform with an SDK, a submission pipeline and outside developers building on it.

## Role and scope

Senior software engineer, working from zero on Shop Minis: the runtime for embedding mini React Native apps inside the main Shop application, the SDK and building blocks partners used, and the ecosystem around submission, upload, maintenance and promotion. I worked directly with the first partner builders in a forward-deployed style — their bug reports were my backlog. I also contributed to Shopify checkout, a surface that renders across a very large number of stores, and spent six months interviewing.

## Context

Shopify runs on high agency: work is self-directed and the quality bar is assumed rather than enforced by process. A prototype has to earn its way into the product, and once it is in the product it inherits consumer-scale expectations immediately.

## Problem

A super-app model breaks most of the assumptions a normal app is built on. Third-party code renders inside your surface, so the platform owns their performance problems and their failure modes. Partners need an SDK that is small enough to learn in an afternoon and complete enough to build something real. And every feature has to work on a low-end device on a slow connection, because a meaningful share of commerce happens there.

## Decisions

The SDK was designed around the components partners actually needed to build a shoppable experience, not around exposing the platform's internals. Fewer, higher-level building blocks meant less support surface and fewer ways to render something that looked broken inside Shop.

For the live-streaming work, the safety mechanics were part of the feature definition rather than a follow-up: kill switches, spam controls, moderation and takedown paths shipped with it. At that scale, the question is not whether something will be abused but how quickly it can be turned off.

Performance targets were set against the slowest realistic device and network rather than the average, because the average hides the users you are actually losing.

## Contribution

Built and shipped parts of the Minis runtime and SDK, worked on the partner-facing developer experience end to end, and helped take the system from a prototype to something the first cohort of external builders shipped on. Contributed to checkout. Interviewed candidates for over six months.

## Outcome

Shop Minis went from zero to a working partner ecosystem, with the first ten building partners shipping into an app that has more than 30 million monthly active users. My role ended in the May 2023 layoffs, along with roughly three thousand other people.

## Learnings

Consumer scale is a different discipline, not a bigger version of the same one. Kill switches, staged rollouts and takedown flows are product features with real design cost, and they are cheapest when designed with the thing they protect.

Building a platform means your users are developers, and developer trust is slow to earn and fast to lose. One breaking change in an SDK costs more goodwill than three good releases earn.
