## Summary

HEGIAS is a Zurich company that makes browser-based 3D and VR for architecture and real estate. When I was there, from July 2020 to April 2021, it sold one product, HEGIAS VR: upload a building model, then walk through it with others in a browser or a headset. The product existed as an MVP, and my job was to help make it something a paying customer could depend on. It was also the job that brought me to Switzerland.

## What I was responsible for

I reported to the CTO and was responsible across the stack: the frontend and CMS, the backend, infrastructure, internal tooling, the component library and integrations. My job was to get the teams aligned and working together, not in silos.

I started the web app and the component library it is built from, and worked with the 3D developers on the layer that draws the scenes.

## What the product looked like

Work is organised as organisation, then project, then scene, and three switchers in the header let you jump between them from anywhere. A scene opens as a docked workspace: the 3D editor with its toolbar, a library of furniture and materials beside it, and tabs for the details, the uploaded files with their processing status, and share links. Behind that sit the parts a paid product needs: sign-in and registration, members with five roles, organisation settings, subscriptions and payment, all in four languages.

The pictures here were recreated in 2026 with invented data. Every organisation, person, building and piece of furniture on screen is made up.

## How it was built

- Web app: React and TypeScript, Ant Design and styled-components, forms with react-hook-form, translations with i18next in German, English, French and Italian.
- Component library: our own React components and icon set, documented in Storybook, so every screen of the app is built from the same cards, chips, inputs and buttons.
- 3D: A-Frame on three.js, with a multiuser layer so people could walk through a scene together.
- Backend: microservices on AWS, with heavy model processing moved out of the request path into background jobs, in-app voice, and integrations with third-party 3D model libraries.
