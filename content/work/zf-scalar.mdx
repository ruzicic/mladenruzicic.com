## Summary

I joined ZF SCALAR in October 2023 as a senior engineer and spent the next two years moving upstream: from feature work, to the technical foundation of a design system, to leading the team that built it, to owning a product. Each step came out of the previous one rather than from a plan.

## Role and scope

Four roles in two years: senior software engineer, UI platform engineer, engineering manager for the UI Platform team, and now Global Product Owner for EVO Touch, ZF's multi-platform application for commercial vehicle drivers. As product owner I own vision, roadmap and delivery, coordinate across three mobile applications in the group, and stay hands-on in feature design and technical trade-offs.

## Context

SCALAR is a large organisation with many product teams building on shared web technology. That structure produces a specific failure mode: every team solves the same interface problems slightly differently, and the differences compound into inconsistency for users and duplicated work for engineers.

## Problem

Arriving on a complex platform migration, the migration itself was not the hard part. The hard part was that UI development workflow and internal tooling made straightforward work slow, and inconsistently so across teams. Nobody owned it, because it was not anyone's feature.

## Decisions

I wrote up the inefficiencies concretely instead of complaining about them, with proposed developer-experience improvements attached. That got the room and the mandate to build a proof of concept.

The design system, Velocity, was built as a product rather than a library. That meant treating adoption as the actual deliverable: talking to product teams before writing components, shipping the pieces they needed next rather than the ones that were interesting to build, and accepting that a component nobody uses is a liability. Accessibility went into the primitives rather than into a checklist, because it is far cheaper there.

I stayed roughly half hands-on as lead engineer while managing the team. On a small platform team serving many consumers, distance from the code makes the prioritisation worse, not better.

## Contribution

Built the proof of concept and the technical foundation. Championed the initiative across the organisation and secured the team and the mandate to pursue it. Acted as technical lead and de facto product owner in the first phase, then as engineering manager: architecture, component API design, roadmap, cross-team communication and adoption work. Then handed it over cleanly and moved into product ownership for EVO Touch.

## Outcome

Velocity moved from an unofficial experiment to the go-to UI solution inside SCALAR and became the standard for new development. By the time I handed it over, roughly 45% of the product teams had adopted it and the initial set of about fifty components was fully taken up by the teams using it. I have since moved off the platform project, and adoption has kept growing without me — which is the outcome you want from a platform. The clearest outcome, though, was organisational: platform work done as product work created the product-ownership role I hold now.

## Learnings

Design systems do not fail on component quality. They fail on adoption strategy, which is a product problem wearing an engineering costume. The teams that had to migrate needed a reason that was true for them this quarter, not an argument about long-term consistency.

Developer experience is product work. It has users, a roadmap, competitors — the copy-pasted local component — and a retention problem. Framing it that way is what made it fundable.
