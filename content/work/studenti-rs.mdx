## Summary

studenti.rs is a Serbian student platform I built in 2008, as a student, for the company I worked at. It kept running. In 2022 I bought it back, rebuilt it, and turned it into a small business I operate almost entirely through automation. It is the longest feedback loop I have on any decision I have ever made.

## Role and scope

Original developer in 2008 at Nemestic. Owner and sole operator since 2022: product, rebuild, infrastructure, moderation policy, monetisation, support and the agent systems that run the day-to-day.

## Context

Students need material — notes, past exams, documents — and a place to ask each other things. The platform accumulated both for well over a decade, along with the search visibility that comes from being the thing that was there first and never went away. That audience is the asset. It is also a moderation liability, because a user-generated document library attracts exactly what you would expect.

## Problem

An old platform carries two debts. The technical one is obvious and boring. The operational one is the real constraint: user-generated content needs review, payments need handling, reports need answering, and a single owner with a full-time job cannot be the queue. Before 2022 the site survived on neglect, which caps what it can be.

## Decisions

Buying it back was the first decision, and the reasoning was simple: the audience and the archive were real and irreplaceable, and the code was not. So the code got replaced.

Monetisation went to PRO subscriptions rather than to more advertising. Ads on a student site pay badly and make the product worse for the people using it most.

Operations were automated rather than staffed. Content approval, triage, scheduling, payments, reimbursements and report handling run through an agent system on infrastructure split between Cloudflare and self-hosted machines at Hetzner. An internal RAG layer with hybrid search gives it the platform's own history to answer from, so decisions are consistent with precedent rather than reinvented each time.

Automation runs on policy, not on vibes. The rules for what is acceptable are written down, the agent applies them, and edge cases escalate to me. Automating a judgment you have not made yourself only produces a faster wrong answer.

## Contribution

Built the original platform in 2008. Bought it, rebuilt it and now run it: architecture, infrastructure, subscriptions, moderation policy, and the operational agents that handle approvals, payments, infrastructure health, dependency updates, bug fixes and analytics.

## Outcome

Eighteen years live, a quarter of a million registered users, tens of thousands of documents, and a subscription product — all operated part-time by one person. Revenue figures stay private.

## Learnings

Longevity is a strategy. A product that simply keeps working for a decade accumulates an audience and a search position that money cannot easily buy.

Operational load, not feature work, is what kills solo products. Every hour of manual review is an hour the product cannot grow, which makes automating operations a product decision rather than an engineering indulgence.
