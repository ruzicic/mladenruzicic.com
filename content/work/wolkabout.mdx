## Summary

WolkAbout made an IoT platform: backend software that connects devices, stores and processes what they send, and WolkReact, the web and mobile apps on top of it for device management, dashboards, rules, messages and reports. Its engineering was in Novi Sad. I came over from Execom in July 2017 and stayed until June 2020, growing from a junior developer into a senior one and, for the last months, a team lead.

## What I was responsible for

Everything frontend, research and development, the marketing website, customer relationships, automations, and the CI/CD and infrastructure tooling.

- I built a modular, micro-frontend Angular application from the ground up, with a focus on performance, scalability and extensibility.
- I built a set of tools to speed up development on the WolkAbout Platform: a Node.js TypeScript REST client and a UI component library in Angular.
- I was the solo developer on the company website.

## What the product looked like

WolkReact opens on a launcher. Each tile is a separate application with its own menu: platform management, administration, devices, data, rules, and visualisation and monitoring. Dashboards are grids of typed widgets, each loading its own data. The same table, filters and detail panel run through devices, points, rules and users, so the modules feel like one product although each one ships on its own.

The pictures were recreated in 2026 with invented data: no campus, building, device or person on screen is real. They were made from a build of April 2021, ten months after I left, so they show the product a little further on than I last saw it, on the same architecture. The 2019 screenshot is the company's own, from its website.

## How it was built

- One Nx monorepo for the whole web front end, with every application module and every shared library its own package under `@wolkabout/`, so modules could be composed per installation and teams could ship without blocking each other.
- Angular and TypeScript, RxJS, NgRx for state, Angular Material, Storybook and Jest.
- Live data arrived as streams over MQTT on a WebSocket. We treated it as streams, throttled and batched at the edge of the app instead of inside components, so a busy device did not redraw a whole dashboard.
- Every screen talks to the platform through the same REST client, `@wolkabout/wolk-rest`, which is still on npm.
