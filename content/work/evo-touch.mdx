## Summary

EVO Touch is ZF's multi-platform application for commercial vehicle drivers. I own its vision, roadmap and delivery, and I act as the product lead for the wider Multi-platform group, where three related mobile applications were solving overlapping problems separately.

## Role and scope

Global Product Owner since May 2025. I set direction and priorities, run delivery with engineers, designers and marketing, and coordinate across the group so that shared capabilities are built once. I stay hands-on where it matters: feature design, technical trade-offs and developer experience. The role came directly out of platform work — the same argument about duplicated effort, one level up.

## Context

Drivers are not office users. They interact with an application in a cab, often in gloves, often in motion, frequently in poor connectivity, in whatever language they speak, and with attention that belongs mostly to the road. Anything that requires care to operate is a design failure rather than a training problem.

## Problem

Three applications in the same group, aimed at adjacent audiences, were duplicating both engineering effort and interaction patterns. Duplication costs twice: teams build the same capability more than once, and drivers learn a slightly different interface each time. Neither cost shows up on any single team's roadmap, which is why it persists.

## Decisions

Priorities are set at group level rather than per app, so that a shared capability is scoped once and adopted rather than reimplemented. That means saying no to locally reasonable requests, with the reason attached.

Consistency is defined in terms of driver-visible behaviour — the same action doing the same thing in the same place — rather than as pixel-identical screens. That is the part users actually feel.

I stay close to implementation. Product decisions about mobile applications degrade quickly when the person making them cannot judge the cost of a trade-off.

## Contribution

Own the roadmap and delivery for EVO Touch. Run the cross-team alignment across the group's mobile applications. Work directly with engineering and design on feature definition and technical trade-offs, and with marketing on how the product is presented to fleets and drivers.

## Outcome

Ongoing. Specific figures, screenshots and roadmap detail are ZF's to publish, so this entry stays at the level of role and approach.

## Learnings

The gap between engineering management and product ownership is smaller than the job titles suggest. Both are prioritisation under uncertainty; the product seat just removes the option of solving a disagreement by writing the code yourself.

Alignment work is invisible when it succeeds. The evidence is a feature that only got built once, which nobody notices.
